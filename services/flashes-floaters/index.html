New flashes or floaters in Fort Myers? Get a careful look at your retina.

A sudden change in your vision deserves a real answer, not a guess. Riverside Family Eye Care is an optometry practice in Fort Myers, and a fresh flash or a shower of new floaters is exactly the kind of thing to call us about. Call us and we will see you as soon as we can.

Request Appointment or call 239-500-2020.

Most floaters are harmless. A sudden change is worth looking at now.

A shower of new floaters drifting across your sight. A flash of light at the edge of your vision, like a camera going off where nothing is there. A curtain or shadow moving in from one side. Floaters are common, and most of them are harmless and settle on their own over time.

But a sudden change is different. A fresh flash, more floaters than usual, or a drifting shadow can be the first sign of a retinal tear or detachment, and when that is what is happening, the safe move is to have it looked at without delay rather than wait it out or guess. That is the whole reason to come in: to find out fast which one it is.

Riverside Family Eye Care is here for Fort Myers families and adults who want thorough, personal eye care and a plain answer on whether a symptom is urgent, not a rushed guess, and not hours in an emergency-room waiting area hoping someone gets to your eyes.

Three things you get by coming in

Prompt attention, a real answer that tells a harmless floater from a tear, and a fast line to a specialist if it turns out you need one.

  • Seen as soon as we can

    A sudden retinal symptom should not wait. Call us and we will see you as soon as we can, while there is still time to act if it turns out to matter.

  • A real answer, not a guess

    Our doctors dilate your eyes and examine the retina, the unhurried kind of look that can tell a harmless floater from a tear or a detachment. This is a proper medical exam, not a screening you get waved through.

  • A fast path to a specialist if you need one

    If the exam shows a tear or a detachment, you are not sent home to make calls. Our doctors coordinate you straight to a retina specialist, so no time is lost in the window when treatment works best.

What happens when you come in

The visit is a proper medical workup, not a quick glance from the doorway. Here is what it covers:

  • We take the history, exactly what you are seeing, in which eye, and when it started, since a new symptom tells a different story than something you have lived with for years.
  • We dilate your eyes for a wide view of the retina and its outer edges, where a tear most often hides.
  • We look for the cause, a tear, a detachment, or a change in the vitreous gel behind the floaters.
  • We explain what we see in plain language, so you leave knowing what is going on and what happens next.

Flashes and floaters are part of the medical eye care our doctors provide.

If the exam turns up a tear or a detachment, treatment is co-managed with a retina surgeon; the surgery itself is done by that specialist, not here. What we own is the urgent exam, the diagnosis, and getting you to the right hands quickly.

Where a sudden symptom should go

A retail optical counter is built to sell frames and fill a prescription, it is not set up to evaluate a sudden retinal symptom. A general emergency room can help in a true crisis, but it often means a long wait and a hand-off to someone who has never seen your eyes before.

Here it works the other way. You reach an optometrist who works with eyes all day, who dilates and examines the retina and tells you plainly whether this is urgent. And if it needs a surgeon, we get you there, you are never left to sort out the next step alone.

Honest answers before you come in

Are floaters always serious?

Usually not. Most are harmless and settle over time. What deserves a prompt look is a sudden shift, a fresh burst of them, a new flash, or a shadow moving across your sight, because those can be the early sign of a tear or detachment.

Should I go to the ER instead?

Call us first. We can give you an eye-specific dilated exam that a general ER often cannot, and we will tell you plainly if the ER or a surgeon is where you actually need to be. If it is a true emergency, we point you there without wasting your time.

Do you do the surgery?

No. We diagnose the problem and coordinate you to a retina specialist for treatment, co-management, not surgery in our office. You get a fast, clear answer here and a direct route to the right hands.

Is it covered by insurance?

A sudden eye symptom is often billable to your medical plan rather than your vision plan. We accept vision and medical insurance plans, listed on our Insurance page, and we are glad to help you check before you come in. Call 239-500-2020 and we will walk through your coverage with you.

Do not wait it out

If you are seeing new flashes, a rush of floaters, or a shadow across your vision, the fastest way in is a phone call, we will find you the soonest slot and have our doctors take a careful look. Care for a sudden eye symptom is usually billable to your medical plan, so call and we will help you check.
